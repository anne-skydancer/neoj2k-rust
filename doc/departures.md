# Departures from OpenJPEG

neo-j2k follows OpenJPEG 2.5.4 on every valid complete stream, and the standard where OpenJPEG is wrong
on cut or invalid input (plan T5). Each departure is listed here with the test that holds it. There is no
compatibility switch. The header fuzz target compares every header read with the oracle's and stops on a
difference that the main header, or a JP2 file's boxes, do not explain by one of DP-5 to DP-8 and DP-11; the
decode fuzz target compares every decode with the oracle's under the same limits (all but those of
streams that would make OpenJPEG allocate a tile-part's declared length, OD-3) and stops on a difference
that is not a refusal for memory (each library counts its own structures against `max_memory`), that the
main header, a tile-part header or the tile-parts' data do not explain by DP-3, DP-5 to DP-8, DP-12 or
DP-14, and that is not DP-2's (samples one decoder clamps to the minimum and the other to the maximum)
(`fuzz/departures.h`). Each check is as narrow as its departure: it names the markers or bytes that make
the two decoders differ, not merely a marker that could. The reference tests leave out the corpus files of `tests/neoj2k-departures.txt`, and
the non-regression differential those of `tests/neoj2k-departures-nonregression.txt`, each tested on its
own.
Departures are numbered DP-n and the oracle's known defects OD-n (`doc/oracle.md`), apart from the
owner's decisions D1-D15 and the fork audit's findings O1-O3. DP-16 to DP-19 are the encoder's (stage 6a); DP-20 is the decoder's again (found in the Rust-twin scoping, 2026-11-20).

## Shared with OpenJPEG: a NaN's payload after the ICT

Not a departure, but a limit of bit identity that both decoders share. The ICT is chosen by the first
component's wavelet (`opj_tcd_mct_decode`; `Tile::decode_samples` here): when it is 9/7, the ICT runs on the
first three components as floats, whatever their own wavelet. A 5/3 component among them holds integers,
which the ICT takes as float bits; some are NaN (0x7F800001-0x7FFFFFFF, 0xFF800001-0xFFFFFFFF). When two
operands of one ICT step are NaN, x86 keeps the payload of the first operand, and which operand comes
first is the compiler's choice: IEEE 754 leaves a NaN's payload unspecified, and C++ compilers treat the
float add and multiply as commutative. The 5/3 component's DC shift then reads the NaN's bits as an
integer, so its sample can differ in the low bits between builds (compilers, sanitizers, the AVX2 and the
scalar kernels). OpenJPEG has the same behaviour: `opj_mct_decode_real` runs SSE on groups of eight and
plain C on the rest, and either may order its operands either way. Such a stream breaks the standard (the
ICT goes with the 9/7 wavelet on all three components, ISO 15444-1 G.3), and no encoder writes it; the
fused output test therefore keeps the ICT to components that are all 9/7 (`decode_unit`,
`test_output_fused`'s 8-bit set).
